AWS European Sovereign Cloud 通过断网演练验证独立运行能力

Inside a successful AWS European Sovereign Cloud exercise to demonstrate independent operation

AI 导读

AWS European Sovereign Cloud 于 2026 年 10 月 24 日完成公开技术演练，关闭 AWS Global Network 骨干网及本地数据传输系统后，将流量改经公共互联网安全路由，在仅使用欧盟内部软硬件和人员的条件下独立运行数小时，期间工作负载持续运行且客户仍可访问数据。

正文 · 原文

On Saturday, October 24, 2026 we conducted a successful exercise demonstrating that the AWS European Sovereign Cloud can operate without depending on any infrastructure outside the European Union (EU). This was the first public technical exercise demonstrating the independence of a sovereign cloud Region.

The AWS European Sovereign Cloud is a new, independent cloud partition for Europe that became generally available in January 2026, with its first AWS Region in Brandenburg, Germany. It is physically and logically separate from other AWS Regions, with no critical dependencies on non-EU infrastructure, and all customer content and customer-created metadata remains in the EU.

The AWS European Sovereign Cloud is managed through dedicated European legal entities established under German law and operated entirely within Europe. My fellow Managing Director, Stefan Hoechbauer, and I called for the exercise. We are EU citizens residing in the EU, responsible for its management and operations, including its infrastructure, technology, and services. Only EU residents located in the EU control its operations and support, and AWS is gradually transitioning the AWS European Sovereign Cloud to be operated exclusively by EU citizens located in the EU.

What we did

In standard operations, the AWS European Sovereign Cloud uses two global systems: the AWS Global Network backbone, and a dedicated system operated by the local EU team to manage limited, controlled transfers of operational AWS data. As we explained ahead of the exercise, neither is critical to running the AWS European Sovereign Cloud. During the exercise, the team switched off both systems, and traffic that normally travels across the AWS Global Network backbone rerouted securely over the public internet.

The AWS European Sovereign Cloud can operate independently at any time, and for several hours on October 24 the team showed exactly that, running it on only its own software and hardware resources, operated by EU-resident staff within the EU.

Workloads continued running, and customers could still access their data. The exercise did not affect service availability within the AWS European Sovereign Cloud, other AWS Regions, or private connectivity through AWS Direct Connect. When the exercise finished, traffic returned to its normal path.

Why we ran it

Transparency is at the core of our digital sovereignty commitment, so we ran the AWS European Sovereign Cloud exercise openly and reported back on the outcome.

The exercise produced verifiable technical and operational evidence that the AWS European Sovereign Cloud can continue to operate independently within the EU. It turns a design claim into a verified result. Organizations in regulated industries and the public sector can take this result to regulators and their boards as evidence for their compliance and assurance documents.
