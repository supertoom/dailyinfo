Executive Summary

This overview highlights recent threat intelligence observed by Unit 42. Common adversary activities focus on strategic intelligence collection and financial extortion. We observed these activities alongside a cross-cutting adoption of artificial intelligence tools across multiple threat disciplines.

Here, we share observations and behaviors associated with these activities.

For in-depth access to Unit 42 threat intelligence insights, explore our Threat Intelligence Services offerings.

Threat Highlights

Cyberespionage

State-sponsored and advanced persistent threat (APT) campaigns focused on covertly acquiring strategic intelligence, intellectual property or geopolitical data.

Key Observations and Behaviors

  • Widespread reliance on living-off-the-land (LotL) binaries and built-in administration tools to evade endpoint defenses
  • Exploitation of zero-day vulnerabilities alongside targeted spear phishing for initial intrusion
  • Covert credential harvesting and low-and-slow data staging designed to sustain long-term operational persistence

Cybercrime: Data Theft, Extortion and Financial Fraud

Organized syndicates and affiliates sought monetary gain through systemic data compromise, extortion and fraudulent transactions.

Key Observations and Behaviors

  • Multi-extortion models combining exfiltration with ransomware deployment or public exposure threats
  • Access acquisition facilitated by infostealer logs, exposed credentials and unpatched perimeter assets
  • Business email compromise (BEC) and payment diversion schemes executing fraudulent wire transfers

AI-Enabled Offensive Operations

Threat actors are integrating generative AI and large language models (LLMs) into their offensive workflows. This notable emerging trend cuts across multiple operational motivations.

Key Observations and Behaviors

  • Generation of highly refined, context-tailored social engineering lures and synthetic personas at scale
  • Utilization of LLMs to assist in rapid scripting, payload debugging and code obfuscation
  • Application of automated reconnaissance and multimodal tools (such as deepfakes) to support executive impersonation and access operations
