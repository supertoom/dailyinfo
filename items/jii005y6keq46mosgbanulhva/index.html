Fast People Search Removal (2026): How to Opt Out & Remove Yourself

正文 · 原文

Anyone can type your name into FastPeopleSearch and see your age, your current address, your phone numbers, and who your relatives are. No account, no fee. Have you checked what it says about you?

The opt-out steps come first, since that’s why you’re here. After that, we’ll look at why the listing is a security risk and how to stop it from creeping back. The steps match the site’s current opt-out form, so you can get your listing taken down today.

How to remove yourself from FastPeopleSearch

Good news first: it’s free, and you don’t need to dig up your own listing beforehand, because FastPeopleSearch finds it from the details you give it.

  1. Go straight to fastpeoplesearch.com/optout. Searching for yourself on the homepage only brings up listings, which won’t get you to the form.
  2. Fill in the first form. Type your first and last name and an email address you can open right away, then tick the consent box and solve the captcha before you hit submit.
  3. Open the email link. FastPeopleSearch then emails you a link to a second form. The link stops working after 24 hours, so it’s easiest to do both forms in one go.
  4. Complete the second form. Add either your date of birth or your street address (you only need one), plus your city, state, and ZIP code, then submit. The form may also ask for a phone number. A confirmation shows up on screen, and another lands in your inbox.

Not sure you’re on the right screen? This fast people search removal walkthrough has screenshots for every step. Two more things make this go smoother:

  • Use a personal email address. Work inboxes often sit behind spam filters that can quietly swallow verification emails.
  • Double-check the second form before you submit it, because there’s no way to edit it afterward. It’s also worth screenshotting the confirmation page.

Why a free people-search listing is a security risk

People usually talk about these sites as a privacy issue. For scammers, though, a listing like this is a cheat sheet. Think about what a fake “bank” caller or a phishing email needs to sound believable: your name, where you live, maybe a relative or two. FastPeopleSearch hands over all of that. Your address and phone number also help someone pose as you when they call customer support about your accounts.

People reported losing $3.5 billion to imposter scams in 2025, according to the FTC, and fake bank alerts were behind some of the most expensive ones. The agency’s guide to family emergency scams also warns that the caller may already know your name and where you live. Would you hang up on a caller who already knew your address and your mother’s name?

When it doesn’t work

If something goes wrong, it’s usually one of these:

  • The email never shows up. Check spam and promotions. Still nothing after half an hour? Start over, since the link may have expired.
  • You’re still listed after 3 days. Search again in a private window, in case your browser is showing an old copy. Then check FastPeopleSearch’s email to see whether the removal worked, and if it didn’t, resend the request carefully. Typos are the usual problem.
  • You have more than one listing. Old addresses or a maiden name can each create a separate profile, and each needs its own request.
  • You still appear on a relative’s profile. Removing your own listing doesn’t take your name off reports about your relatives or neighbors, so check those too.
  • It’s gone from FastPeopleSearch but still on Google. That’s an old copy Google hasn’t updated yet. You can ask Google to refresh outdated content by pasting in the old listing’s link.

Why your listing comes back

Here’s the annoying part. FastPeopleSearch doesn’t stop collecting after you opt out. It keeps pulling in public records and commercial data, so a move, a new phone number, a home purchase, or even an updated voter registration can put a fresh profile back online, sometimes months later.

Is any of this legal? In the US, mostly yes, although some state privacy laws give residents more say, such as the right to stop a company from selling their data. So don’t think of removal as a one-off. Search your name every few months and repeat the opt-out if you pop up again.

It’s not the only site with your data

Opting out of FastPeopleSearch only covers FastPeopleSearch. Lots of similar sites use the same kinds of records, and each has its own opt-out form. Search your name on two or three big ones, like TruePeopleSearch, Spokeo, or Whitepages, and opt out wherever you show up. It’s tedious, but every removal means one less copy that can leak.

Leaks do happen. In 2024, 2.9 billion records from background-check firm National Public Data ended up on a hacking forum. It was one of the biggest leaks in recent years, but far from the only one.

Conclusion

That’s really all there is to it. You know how to get your FastPeopleSearch listing removed and how to spot it when it comes back. Leave it up, and a scammer gets your address, phone number, and family names for free.

So do the opt-out today, and set a reminder to check again in three months. It’s ten minutes well spent.
