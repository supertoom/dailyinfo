Threat actors exploited several Mitel MiCollab vulnerabilities as zero-days in a data theft campaign, according to Accenture’s Apex Labs. CISA on Wednesday added five MiCollab flaws to its Known Exploited Vulnerabilities (KEV) catalog. 

In one attack observed by Accenture, hackers gained privileged access to a MiCollab server and quickly reached the targeted organization’s identity infrastructure, ultimately stealing sensitive business information.

The MiCollab business collaboration platform enables employees to communicate through calls, messages, and virtual meetings. Accenture noted that the product’s frequent internet exposure and integration with Active Directory make it particularly attractive to hackers.

Mitel on November 16 announced patches for five vulnerabilities affecting the product’s audio, web and video conferencing component, including arbitrary file upload, XML injection, unauthorized access, command injection, and authentication bypass. They can all be exploited by unauthenticated attackers, and two critical bugs allow arbitrary command execution. 

The security holes are tracked as CVE-2026-133275, CVE-2026-133303, CVE-2026-133311, CVE-2026-133323, and CVE-2026-133324.

Accenture found evidence that the vulnerabilities were exploited in the wild before Mitel published its advisory. The company determined that threat actors deployed a web shell on a victim’s systems on November 15.

According to Accenture, the attackers executed commands on a MiCollab server just 12 minutes after the first request, and gained root privileges within half an hour.

The hackers harvested additional credentials and, roughly 20 hours after their first observed request, gained access to internal backup and database servers.

The investigation uncovered evidence that some of the malicious activity may have been driven by AI tools, with the attacker’s infrastructure communicating with two unnamed commercial AI API services. 

“Because the telemetry is sampled, Apex Labs cannot determine precisely how the threat actor used the services. However, the observed activity is consistent with potential uses such as data triage, operational support for exploitation or web-shell activity, and preparation of extortion material,” Accenture noted.

It added that communications between the attackers’ infrastructure and the AI services resumed — after an eight-hour silence — at precisely the same second that the hackers sent an authentication bypass request to the targeted MiCollab server.

The company also found evidence of compromise at a second victim, with a MiCollab server communicating with the same attacker’s infrastructure since November 9. However, Accenture could not establish how the second server was hacked, as it had been affected by older vulnerabilities as well.

The researchers have not linked the attacks to a known cybercrime group, but believe they may have been part of an extortion campaign. 

Mitel has released patches, but its advisory — last updated on November 24 — does not mention in-the-wild exploitation of the vulnerabilities.

Accenture warned organizations that attackers may have compromised their MiCollab servers before the fixes were installed and urged them to check for indicators of compromise (IoCs).

CISA 已将全部五个漏洞添加到了其 KEV 目录中，指示联邦机构在 11 月 28 日前修复其中四个；最后一个的截止日期为 12 月 9 日。 

威胁行为者经常针对 Mitel 产品。CISA 的 KEV 目录目前包含十二个此类漏洞。
